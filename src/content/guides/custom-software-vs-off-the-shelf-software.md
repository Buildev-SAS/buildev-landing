**Off-the-shelf software** (a SaaS product or a licensed program) is built for many companies at once. **Custom software** is built around the processes of a single one. Neither is better in every case: the decision depends on how standard the process you want to solve is.

## Quick comparison

| Criteria | Off-the-shelf software | Custom software |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to get started | Immediate | Weeks or months |
| Upfront cost | Low | Higher |
| Long-term cost | Per-user licenses that grow with the company | Hosting and maintenance |
| Fit with your processes | Your company adapts to the software | The software adapts to your company |
| Integrations | Limited to what the vendor offers | Whatever you need |
| Ownership | The vendor | Defined by contract with your company |
| Competitive advantage | The same one your competitors have | Your own |

## When to choose off-the-shelf software

- The process is **standard** and does not set you apart: accounting, payroll, email, office tools.
- You need a solution **by tomorrow**.
- The tool covers almost everything you need **without customization**.
- The number of users is small and licensing costs are reasonable.

## When to choose custom software

- The process **is your advantage**: the way you sell, serve customers, ship or quote is what sets you apart.
- Your team lives in **spreadsheets, copy-paste and chat messages** to cover what your current software can't do.
- You need to **connect several systems** (payments, WhatsApp, inventory, invoicing) into a single workflow.
- Per-user licenses are already a significant cost and keep growing.
- You want to **sell the software** as a product or service to your own customers.

## The middle ground: combine both

In practice, many companies use off-the-shelf tools for standard needs and build custom software only for what gives them an edge, connecting everything through integrations. That way, you invest where it actually creates value.

## A simple test to decide

Make a list of the tasks your team does **outside** your current software: reports built by hand, data copied from one system to another, follow-ups in chat. If that list is long and takes up many hours a week, generic software is already costing you more than it seems.
